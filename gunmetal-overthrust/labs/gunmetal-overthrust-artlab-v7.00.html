What to judge in v2.00. The new mechs: chunky, vibrant, super deformed, after the SD suits you shared (their big heads, fat feet and heavy guns, their cast shadows and hot rim light), made our own: the cobalt VANGUARD with its SNAIL shield and drum rifle, the hazard orange BULWARK with its slab shield, shoulder cannon and gatling. ACTION plays each animation on the real mover (the same code the control lab runs): IDLE breathes, WALK strides in eight frames, JUMP squats, rises, tucks and lands with a bounce, DASH leans in with afterimages, HOVER burns downward, FIRE MG flashes, kicks and throws casings, CANNON charges and blasts, MISSILES pop the shoulder pods. AIM tilts the gun level, up or down. PAN walks the camera along the street; drag the frame to scrub it.

What to judge in v3.00. Your second round of notes. Both mechs are redrawn bigger and chunkier (the VANGUARD 50 px tall and the BULWARK 53, up from 45 and 44, and far wider, near the texel count of the SD references you shared) in seven-step ramps that shift hue from navy shadows to teal highlights (orange: maroon to pale gold), with big light clusters, a dome helmet whose eye scans the visor, and a crouched, chunky stance. They move on keyframes that play smoothly at any speed: a three-second breathing idle where the torso leads and the head, gun and shield follow a beat late, a trot with planted boots and dust at every footfall. WEAPON (the slot row) gives mech 1 any of four weapons, each iconic and animated its own way: the RC-9 drum rifle at the hip (the bolt throws brass, the drum turns), the GR-4 gatling (it spins up, fires a long flame, drops belt links, its barrels glow red when hot), the HB-9 bazooka riding the shoulder (a heavy rocket, a back-blast of fire and smoke, an empty tube, a reload), the HX heat blade (its molten edge crawls at rest; FIRE MAIN swings it with a smear and a lunge). SHOULDER fires the VANGUARD's missile pods or the BULWARK's HC-1 cannon.

What to judge in v7.00: ROUTE A2 OF THE ART BAKE-OFF. The other routes and the verdict come on the Studio Head's judging page.

What to judge in v6.00: THE STREET EXPERIMENT. The lab opens on the street as a living scene, written in code only, on the one 480x270 grid and the one palette, with the VANGUARD standing in it: the studio's test of whether code can carry the world layer (doc 16, section 7). Rain falls in three depths (fine and dim far off, heavier near, a few long streaks in front), leaning on the wind; haze drifts across the far and middle city so each layer sits a step nearer the sky; windows live (a light goes off, another comes on, a few rooms watch television); the neon sign buzzes; the street lamps flicker, about one in three failing, and their light shows in the rain falling through it, in their pools and in the streaks they throw down the wet road; smoke rises from the chimneys and bends on the wind; the rain splashes on the road, rings the puddles and bounces off the mech's shoulders. Judge it as a place: does it feel like a city at night in the rain, and does the mech still read first? The mood (doc 16 section 3; the canvas names it under the badge): time night, about 23:40; weather steady rain on a light wind from the west; key light the moon, high on the right (+x), cold blue white, on the same side as the sun of the other lights, so every rim and lit edge keeps its side; glow colour sodium amber, the street lamps and the lit windows. STREET switches LIVING and STILL (the street as before), WEATHER switches RAIN and CLEAR, and LIGHT shows the same street at DUSK and SUNSET (the lamps are dark at sunset, as before). The six world sliders tune it live (RAIN, WIND, HAZE, SMOKE, FLICKER, WINDOWS); COPY VALUES gives the numbers to paste back to this seat. The palette gate below runs on the living street as on the still one; a gate in the chain (WORLDTEST) holds the living street to one grid, one palette and the readability gate (the mech's outline stands apart from the street in every light, at five spots, rain falling). The grid, the scheme and the scale stay your picks.

What to judge in v4.00. Your third round of notes. Both mechs are scaled up: the VANGUARD stands 64 px (about 18 metres at the street's scale, a Gundam's height) and the BULWARK 62, shorter but broader. Every part has one place and one purpose, head to torso to weapon. The VANGUARD: a gold swept crest, a visor slot that steps down at the front (a determined brow) where the cyan eye scans, a white jaw, a chest with a core lens that breathes, the pods pauldron, the spiral shield with twin boosters peeking over it, white knee caps and toes. The BULWARK: a turret helmet with a black T visor on a steel mask and an amber eye, a barrel chest with a grille and a collar of chasing lamps, a flared hover skirt, a riveted pauldron, the HC-1 cannon riding its shoulder. Every hand weapon now hangs its whole arm from under the pauldron, so the arm, the fist and the gun read apart from the body; all four weapons are redrawn bigger. On your note about the dark outline: a second rim, the sky's light, now edges the back and the top of each mech in each grade's own colour (magenta at sunset, violet at dusk, moonlight blue at night), and at night only the paint darkens, never the gunmetal. Measured on this street, the worst spot at NIGHT now keeps 66% of the outline apart from the backdrop (it was 44%). Try NIGHT and PAN along the street.

The street, at the mechs' scale. A mech stands about 13 metres tall (45 rows: 17% of the 480 grid's height, 13% of the 640's), so the street is drawn at about 3.5 pixels to the metre: cars, a bus, barriers, cones, hydrants, lamp posts and trees at human size, fire escapes, shop signs, air conditioners, pipes, puddles and manholes after the detail of the Metal Slug backdrops you shared. Next to them the mechs read massive.

640x360 shows the same parts. Every part is drawn for the 480 grid, so at 640 the mech stands 56 of 360 rows (16%) instead of 56 of 270 (21%) and more city shows. Choosing 640 means redrawing every part about a third larger; 480 is the lean one, chunkier and cheaper to draw.

The palette gate (pitch section 6, rules 1 and 2) reads the canvas back after every change: every device pixel must be one of the level palette's colours under the active light, and every k x k block must be one colour, so nothing was smoothly scaled. The swatches show the palette; the lit ones are on screen now; the ringed one is the player's reserved rim light.

The light. One key light, the sun on the right: every lit building edge and the player's rim face it. SUNSET, DUSK and NIGHT are palette grades of the same pixels; night also darkens the paint one step inside its own ramp and turns the rim cool.
